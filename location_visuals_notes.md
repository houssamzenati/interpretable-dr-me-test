## What the figures mean

**Prototype introduction.** An outcome-space location `v` defines a similarity
profile `y -> k(y,v)`. Gaussian contours show a soft neighbourhood: distant
outcomes still contribute, with smaller weights. The two bars are exact
population expectations under the known synthetic mixture distributions.
Their signed difference is the witness `w(v)`; neither bar is a density or a
probability of belonging to a hard neighbourhood.

**Location learning.** Fix the first location `v1`, then evaluate each candidate
second location `v2`. The heatmap shows the joint estimated DR criterion
`mean(z)^T (cov(z) + tau I)^(-1) mean(z)`, for the two corrected witness
coordinates. It measures the combined signal relative to its covariance.
It is not a map of `w(v2)`, a density difference, or an individual causal effect.
The selected location maximizes this criterion over the evaluated grid;
it is not a certified optimum over the continuous outcome space.

The two scenarios share the same absolute colour scale, without quantile clipping.
Changing `v1` changes which `v2` complements it. A useful second coordinate may
have a small raw witness because its covariance with the first coordinate matters.
The oracle maximizer is omitted to keep this explanation focused.

## Scientific details retained

- `q` means treatment arm 1 and uses violet `#70469A`.
- `q'` means treatment arm 0 and uses rose `#AC527A`.
- The witness and corrected scores use the sign arm 1 minus arm 0.
- Gaussian `kernel_scale` in the original code is the **squared bandwidth**.
- The original plotted Hotelling objective includes `n_train`. The new learning
  criterion divides it out, matching manuscript Section 4. Maximizers are unchanged.
- Outcome clouds display synthetic interventional samples, not observed treatment
  groups. Exact population bars explain the target; observational estimation uses
  only `(X,A,Y)` through the estimated DR scores.
- In this particular toy generator, treatment depends on `X`, but the potential
  outcomes are generated independently of `X`. There is no outcome confounding.
  This example illustrates prototype meaning and learning geometry; it does not
  demonstrate confounding removal.
- The generator cross-fits nuisances within the learning portion and reserves
  unused observations. It draws a training landscape, not a calibrated final test.
  The manuscript's full procedure uses separate nuisance, learning, and testing
  splits; the selected locations are frozen before testing independent data.
- A fixed finite set of locations can miss a distributional difference. Learning
  seeks informative coordinates, not a guarantee of universal detection.
- The manuscript's efficiency claim concerns the finite signal's canonical-gradient
  covariance. It does not claim a uniformly most powerful distributional test.

## Intended outputs

The script writes to `results/location_explainer/`:

- `prototype_similarity.pdf` and its PNG: one prototype and two mean similarities.
- `location_learning.pdf`: two pages, one per fixed-first-location scenario.
- `location_learning_reveals.pdf`: six pages, three reveals per scenario.
- Corresponding PNGs, an optional GIF, `figure_data.npz`, and `metadata.json`.

The numerical archive and metadata preserve locations, computed quantities,
parameters, colour mapping, and provenance so the explanation is reproducible.

## Placement and spoken explanation

**Before the operational chain:** show the prototype figure.
“A prototype is a reference outcome. We compare how similar outcomes are to it
under the two interventions. The difference between these averages is the witness
at this prototype. In observational data, these averages must be estimated with
causal adjustment.”

**After step 4, learning:** show one heatmap scenario at a time.
“Keep the first prototype fixed. Each point here is a possible second prototype.
The colour measures how informative the pair is, accounting for their uncertainty
and correlation. We select the best grid candidate. Changing the first prototype
changes what is useful as the second.”
